Gil Morales

O'Neil Disciples · "Short-Selling with the O'Neil Disciples"
  • Rule 1 — market first: short only in a clear bear trend, as early in the cycle as possible. The Nifty regime banner applies this.
  • Rule 2 — short former leaders: big winners of the prior bull run, 8-12 weeks off their peak (or immediate LSFB failures).
  • Rule 3 — liquidity: only stocks trading big daily value; risk is inversely correlated to liquidity.
  • Entry: high-volume break of the 50-day MA; or a wedging rally back into a declining 50-day that fails on volume.
  • Climax runs: 3-5 up-days with gaps and the widest daily ranges = distribution; short the first reversal.
  • Risk: predetermined stop, cover into panic/weak markets, take 20-30% profit targets, no trailing stops.

Qullamaggie

Kristjan Kullamägi · Parabolic Short
  • Candidate: up 50-100%+ in days/weeks (large caps) on 3-5+ consecutive up days.
  • Never day one of the vertical move — wait for the crack; the amateur shorts get run over.
  • Trigger: break of the opening-range low, first red 5-minute candle, or a failed VWAP bounce (first crack → weak bounce → fail).
  • Stop: high of the day, or VWAP reclaim.
  • Target: cover into the 10/20-day MAs — the "home" these stocks bounce to.
  • Risk 0.25-1% per trade; short-only setups, never two-way on the same name.

Martin Luk

USIC Champion · short-at-resistance framework
  • Short resistance, not breakdowns: higher risk-reward and win rate than chasing breaks.
  • Where: declining 21/50 EMAs and declining anchored VWAP when they converge — the first bounce into them is the short.
  • 23 & 50 EMA > 9 EMA for the shorting zone; the 9 works only as an add.
  • Weak stock in a weak sector — never the laggard inside a strong sector.
  • Multi-timeframe echo: weekly resistance, daily weakness, 15-min/1-min confirmation (break of reversal-candle low).
  • Holds 2-3 days; trim into acceleration; tight stops ~2-3%; risk ~0.5% per trade.

Simon Russo

Market Wizards: The Next Generation · pseudonym
  • Short parabolic rallies in structurally weak companies — for India: dilution risk (QIP/FPO pipeline), promoter pledge, high cash burn.
  • Trigger is capitulation: volume and price both accelerating to extremes — the rubber band about to snap.
  • Not picking tops: get short in the vicinity and let risk management do the rest.
  • Constant-risk pyramiding: start small, add as the stock rallies against you, lowering the stop so total dollar risk stays flat (~1%).
  • Cover into the panic drop — where the buyers are exhausted. Shorts last 1-3 days; longer = warning.
  • Broken short = flip long: if it grinds up with no capitulation phase, get out and reassess.

Short score & market regime

Score (0-100) = trend structure (below 50 EMA +10, below 200 EMA +10, bear stack +15) + triggered setups (parabolic +15, first crack / climax / 50-DMA break / wedge / bounce +10 each, distribution / low break / gap / ex-leader +5 each, capped) + relative strength vs Nifty over 3 months (≤-10pp +15, ≤-5pp +10, <0 +5) + volume confirmation (≥2× +10, ≥1.5× +5). Candidates = at least one setup tag and 30-day average turnover above the filter.

Regime (Morales Rule 1): Nifty below both the 50 and 200 EMA = BEAR — shorts favoured; below the 50 only = CORRECTION — short at resistance only; 4+ index distribution days = DISTRIBUTING — prepare the watchlist; otherwise BULL — avoid fresh shorts. The banner at the top of the screener computes this nightly from ^NSEI.

India execution notes: intraday naked shorts must be squared off same-day unless you borrow via SLB; swing shorts are usually taken via stock futures. Cover costs, carry and squeeze risk before sizing — Russo's constant-risk pyramid only works with a hard stop that survives gap-ups.
